"Data is an asset" is the most repeated sentence in the field and usually the emptiest. It means something only when it changes a decision, and the test is the one you would apply to a building or a fleet: an asset has an owner, a recorded value, a maintenance cost and a lifecycle that ends. A table nobody owns, nobody can value and nobody will ever delete is not an asset. It is storage. DMBOK defines governance as authority over the management of data assets, so the word is doing real work in that definition.

**In practice.** Start with the ten datasets that feed executive reporting and answer four questions for each: who owns it, what is it worth, what does it cost us when it is wrong, and when does it go away. Record the answers in the inventory or catalog. The useful measure is the share of assets that have both an owner and an approved definition, and the trend in that number.

**Where it goes wrong.** A crawler fills the catalog with every table in the estate, and everything it found is now called an asset. Nobody knows what percentage has an owner. Meanwhile nothing is ever retired, because deleting feels risky and keeping costs nothing visible. An asset register that only ever grows is an inventory of liabilities.
