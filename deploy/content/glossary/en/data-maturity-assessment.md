The model is the ruler; the assessment is the act of measuring with it. You pick a framework, gather evidence across its dimensions — quality, stewardship, metadata, security, architecture — and map each one to a level, so the result reads "level 1 on quality, level 3 on security" rather than a single grade. The useful version has four moves: scope it, gather evidence, map the levels, and turn the gaps into a plan with owners and dates. It is a baseline you repeat, not a certificate you frame.

**In practice.** Evidence beats questionnaires. Ask to see the glossary, the last few incident tickets, the access approval trail, and score what is shown rather than what is described. Keep the scope to questions you will act on: a quick read to find your weakest dimension is useful, a forty-item scored baseline across every knowledge area is a project in itself. And you do not need one to find pain — interviews and a year of escalations will surface that faster.

**Where it goes wrong.** The assessment becomes the program's first six months and its only deliverable. Or the target gets written as a level, "reach level 3", which nobody outside the data team can evaluate and no budget review will fund. Translate each gap into a business result before it goes on the roadmap.
