A framework is the parts list for governance. Most reduce to four components: roles and responsibilities (owners, stewards, custodians), policies and standards, decision bodies such as a council, and oversight that measures whether any of it is happening. Reference frameworks like DAMA-DMBOK and DCAM describe what good looks like across the whole discipline. Your own framework is the version of that structure you have chosen to build. It is not the operating model, which says who in your organization fills each part and how decisions actually move.

**In practice.** Borrow the vocabulary early and the scope late. Using the standard words for owner, steward and critical data element costs nothing and settles a category of arguments. Sequencing is a different matter: a framework presents its areas as peers, and the order you build them in should come from where your escalations come from, not from the chapter order.

**Where it goes wrong.** The framework diagram becomes the deliverable. It gets a slide, a charter and a launch, and every box on it is filled with a role nobody holds. A framework tells you what a complete program contains. Until the boxes have names in them, nothing has been governed.
