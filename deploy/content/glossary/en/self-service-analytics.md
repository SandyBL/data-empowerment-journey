Self-service analytics replaces the ticket queue with infrastructure: a searchable catalog to find data, access provisioned by role rather than by email thread, certified datasets to build on, and BI tools business people can actually use. The point is speed to an answer for the routine question — the store manager checking local stock, the marketing analyst slicing last month's campaign — while the central team stops being the bottleneck for every report change and starts maintaining the things everyone else builds on.

**In practice.** It works on top of governance, not instead of it. The minimum is a small set of certified datasets with a named owner, agreed definitions for the metrics people will compute, and access tiers that let most users into aggregated operational data without an approval each time. Then training on the users' own data, because a tool nobody can interpret just produces confident wrong numbers faster.

**Where it goes wrong.** The tool is rolled out before any of that. Every department builds its own dashboards with its own formula for the same metric, the reports multiply, and within a year the leadership meeting has three revenue figures and trusts none of them. That is not a failure of self-service. It is self-service without a glossary.
