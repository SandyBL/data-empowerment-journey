A catalogue is a search engine for your own data. It answers the two questions that otherwise consume analyst time — does a dataset for this already exist, and can I trust it — by putting technical structure, business definitions and ownership in one place. Most catalogues can harvest structure automatically from warehouses, BI tools and pipelines; none can supply meaning, which is why a catalogue is a governance project wearing a tooling budget.

**In practice.** Catalogue adoption tracks a single number: the share of assets people actually search for that have an owner and a definition. Populating the top 200 assets well beats populating 20,000 emptily, and it is the version that gets used twice.

**Where it goes wrong.** The catalogue is bought before the definitions exist and rolled out as the programme's deliverable. Six months later it holds every column in the estate, 3% have a description, and the organisation concludes that catalogues do not work. The tool worked fine; there was nothing to put in it.
