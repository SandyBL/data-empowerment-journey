TDWI, the data and analytics research and education organization, describes analytics maturity as five stages that most organizations recognize in themselves on first reading. Nascent: data use is ad hoc and individual, and reporting is manual. Emerging: leadership starts asking for evidence, and definitions are inconsistent in a way everyone knows about. Developing: training is formalized and data is consulted routinely in operational decisions. Mature: fluency reaches well beyond the technical teams and standard definitions are the default. Leading: evidence is expected, and analytics is a competitive input rather than a support function. It looks at analytics as a whole — organization, infrastructure, data management, analytics and governance — rather than at governance alone.

**In practice.** Use it for the conversation about how decisions get made, where a governance-only model would miss the point; it pairs naturally with literacy and culture work. The stage descriptions are short enough to read out in a leadership meeting, and people place themselves quickly, usually more generously than the evidence supports.

**Where it goes wrong.** The stage label becomes the objective. "Move from developing to mature" is not a business outcome and will not survive a budget review. "Cut the competing revenue definitions from four to one" is the same progress in a form someone will fund. The stage is a diagnostic, not a destination.
