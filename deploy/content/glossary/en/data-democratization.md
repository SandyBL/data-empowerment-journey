The opposite of data tyranny, the state where a routine question needs a ticket, a week and written permission from IT. Democratization moves the default from "locked unless approved" to "available unless risky", with ownership pushed out to the business domains and a central function that sets security standards, maintains the glossary and keeps the architecture coherent. It is not an ungoverned free-for-all. Done properly, it takes more governance than the lockdown did, just of a different kind.

**In practice.** Tier the data. Highly restricted data — PII, financial ledgers, trade secrets — keeps strict approvals, masking and audit logging. Operational business data is open to authenticated users through certified models. Raw exploratory data lives in sandboxes. Add a catalog so people can find things, role-based provisioning so access does not depend on somebody's inbox, and literacy training so access turns into decisions.

**Where it goes wrong.** It is announced as a value and delivered as a licence: everybody gets the BI tool, nobody gets definitions or certified data. Or the restrictions stay, people route around them, and the organization ends up with a data black market of exported spreadsheets — democratization, technically, with none of the controls.
