Change management is the part of governance that deals with people rather than data. Governance redistributes authority, adds routines and asks people to stop doing things that work for them locally, such as exporting raw files into their own spreadsheets. Resistance to that is normal, and it usually has ordinary causes: people do not know what is changing, they fear losing control of their own numbers, or they see friction with no benefit to them. The DMBOK gives the topic its final chapter, which is roughly where most programs leave it too.

**In practice.** Open with a business story, not a policy: the forecast that was wrong, the campaign that went out late. Recruit champions inside the domains, stewards and team leads the rest of the team already listens to, because peers persuade where a central team cannot. Translate jargon into plain sentences, make early wins visible, and measure adoption: how many people use certified datasets each week, and how many shadow spreadsheets have been retired.

**Where it goes wrong.** It gets treated as communications: a launch email, a policy PDF, a slide at the town hall. Everyone is informed and nobody behaves differently. Counting published policies measures how much was written. It tells you nothing about whether anyone changed what they do.
