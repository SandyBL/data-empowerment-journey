An operating model has four parts: the domains and their owners, the roles and how much time each carries, the forums and what each one decides, and the routes — how a definition gets changed, how an issue gets escalated, how an exception gets approved. It also declares a shape: centralized, federated, or a hub with domain teams. Federated is the most common answer and the least often specified, which is why it so often means nobody.

**In practice.** Fit the model to the organization you actually have. A company that runs everything through functional heads will not sustain a domain-based model, no matter how correct the domain map is. Governance that fights the existing decision structure loses.

**Where it goes wrong.** The model is designed for the end state and launched all at once — every domain, every role, every forum, on the same date. Capacity does not exist, the forums do not fill, and eighteen months later the model is a document that describes a thing that never happened. Two domains done properly beats twelve announced.
