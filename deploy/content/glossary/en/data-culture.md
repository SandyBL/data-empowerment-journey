Culture is observable in small behaviours. Whether a meeting starts with the number or with the opinion. Whether an analyst can say the data does not support the plan without a career cost. Whether a report that shows a failure gets circulated or gets revised. Whether people go looking for existing data before commissioning new. None of that comes from a poster; all of it comes from what senior people are seen to do when the data is inconvenient.

**In practice.** Culture moves when the incentives move. The most effective single intervention is a leader publicly changing a decision because of evidence, and saying that is why. That is worth more than a year of communications.

**Where it goes wrong.** Culture is treated as the programme's soft, unmeasurable component and handed to communications. It is neither soft nor unmeasurable: count how often the numbers in a decision paper are reconciled beforehand, how many decisions cite a source, how long it takes to answer "where did this come from". Those move, and they can be tracked.
