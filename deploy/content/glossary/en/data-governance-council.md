A council exists for cross-domain conflict. Two functions want incompatible definitions of the same entity; a policy would cost one department a quarter's roadmap; a domain wants to expose data that another domain is regulated on. Those cannot be resolved inside a domain, and escalating each one to an executive committee does not scale. The council is the standing place where they are resolved, by the people accountable for the domains involved.

**In practice.** A council that works is small — the data owners, chaired by someone with executive weight — meets monthly, and works from a queue of specific decisions with a proposed answer attached. Minutes record what was decided and by whom, which is what makes it authority rather than discussion.

**Where it goes wrong.** The council becomes a status meeting. Twenty attendees, a deck, a round of updates, no decisions, and within two quarters the owners send delegates instead of attending. The reliable test: if the agenda does not contain the word "approve", cancel the meeting and write an email.
