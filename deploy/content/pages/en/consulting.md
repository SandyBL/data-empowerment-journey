Most governance consulting fails in a predictable way. It produces a framework document, an ambitious target operating model and a three-year roadmap, all of which are correct and none of which anybody is accountable for on Monday. Eighteen months later the framework is a file, the roadmap has been superseded, and the organisation concludes that data governance does not work here.

I sell the opposite of that. Every engagement below has one thing in common: it ends with named people holding named decisions, written down, with the first few already made.

## How I work

Three pillars run through everything, and they are the same three the [framework on the homepage](/en/#framework) describes.

**Governance foundations — the compass.** The decision rights, the domains and their owners, the policies that are short enough to be enforced and the standards that are specific enough to be tested. This is the part that is genuinely reusable from the DMBOK, and the part most often over-produced.

**Use case — the engine.** Governance with no business consequence has no sponsor and does not survive a budget cycle. So every engagement is anchored to something the business already wants: a regulatory finding to close, a report nobody trusts, a launch blocked by a data dependency, an AI initiative that cannot answer what it trained on.

**Change management — the heart.** The reason most of this work fails. New behaviour has to be easier than the old behaviour, visibly backed by someone senior, and rewarded at least once in public. If that is not designed in, the rest is documentation.

## Engagement shapes

Each of these is a real shape I deliver, not a menu of hours. Scope, duration and output are agreed before we start, and each one ends with something you own.

- **Governance diagnostic — two to three weeks.** Interviews across the domains that matter, profiling of the data behind your disputed reports, and an assessment against the DAMA capability areas. Output: a written diagnosis, a ranked list of what is actually blocking you, three costed quick wins, and a maturity read you can put in front of an executive committee. This is the right start when the organisation knows something is wrong and disagrees about what.
- **Operating model design — six to ten weeks.** Domains, owners, stewards, forums, decision rights and the routes between them, designed for the organisation you have rather than the one an org chart implies. Output: the operating model document, a populated decision-rights grid, role descriptions with an honest time commitment attached, terms of reference for the governance council, and the first council meeting facilitated with a real agenda.
- **Data quality practice — eight to twelve weeks, one domain.** Critical data elements derived from consequence, standards written to be machine-checkable, rules implemented with thresholds and owners, and a report that names the process that produced each breach. Output: a working quality practice on one domain, a cost-of-poor-quality baseline, and a pattern the next domain can copy without me.
- **Embedded advisory — monthly, ongoing.** For organisations with an internal governance lead who needs a second opinion rather than a delivery team. A fixed block of hours per month: reviewing artefacts before they go to a committee, preparing difficult conversations, being the external voice when an internal recommendation needs weight behind it.

## What I do not do

I do not sell tool selection as a governance engagement. I will happily tell you which capability you are missing and what class of tool covers it, but if the definitions do not exist, a catalogue purchase will not create them, and I would rather say so before the invoice than after.

I also do not take engagements where the sponsor will not commit an owner. Governance without a business owner who can approve a definition and say no to another function is an exercise, and the outcome is predictable enough that it is not worth either of our time.

## Pricing shape

Diagnostics and fixed-scope design engagements are quoted as a fixed fee against a written scope, so you are not buying an open-ended day rate. Advisory is a monthly block of hours. Workshops are priced per session, and the [workshops page](/en/workshops/) covers what is included — including the private, branded simulator space and the facilitator report.

Everything is delivered remotely, in English, Spanish or Portuguese, and I work alongside an internal lead wherever one exists.
