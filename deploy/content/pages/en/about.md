Hello. I am **Sandy Bradbury**. I design and implement data governance frameworks that are practical and built around the business, aligned to the international **DAMA** standard without ever being handed over as a copy of it.

Very little of the work turns out to be about technology. Most of it is change management, and the daily habits of the people who touch data. A framework is worth as many people as behave differently because of it, and that is a harder thing to count than published policies.

## What I actually do

Most of my work is one of four things: writing the operating model that says who decides what about which data, getting a business glossary to the point where it settles arguments instead of collecting terms, building a data quality practice that measures what somebody will act on, and running the workshops that make the first three stick.

In all four, the work is the decisions rather than the diagrams. An organization rarely needs a better target-state architecture. It needs one named person who can approve a definition, one forum where cross-domain conflicts get settled, and a written record of what was decided. None of that is glamorous. All of it changes the outcome.

## Certifications and DAMA

I hold the **CDMP** (Certified Data Management Professional) credentials from **DAMA International**: the Associate level and the Data Management Fundamentals examination. DAMA is the professional body behind the DMBOK, the reference framework most large governance programs are measured against, and the certification is its practitioner standard.

I mention this for one reason: when a governance recommendation is contested inside an organization, it helps that the framework behind it is an international standard with a body of practice behind it, rather than one consultant's preference. The DMBOK is a starting point rather than a delivery plan, but it is one everyone in the room can check.

## Why this site exists

I kept giving the same explanations. What data governance is and is not. Why a data owner has to be a business leader with budget. Why a maturity score is a diagnosis rather than an objective. Why the catalog nobody filled in is not the tool's fault.

So this site is where I write them down properly: [articles](/en/blog/), a [glossary](/en/glossary/), free [templates](/en/templates/) and [playbooks](/en/playbooks/), a [cost-of-bad-data calculator](/en/calculator/), and three scenario simulators that put a visitor inside the trade-offs instead of describing them: [Data Governance Day-to-Day](/simulators/en/data-governance-day-to-day/), [Who Owns This?](/simulators/en/data-ownership-conflict/) and [Data Literacy](/simulators/en/data-literacy/). All of it is published in English, Spanish and Portuguese, because governance conversations happen in the language of the people having them.

The consulting is downstream of that. If the writing is useful, some readers will want the work done rather than explained. If it is not, the writing was still worth doing.
