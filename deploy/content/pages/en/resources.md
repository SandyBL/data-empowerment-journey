Every download on this site is free, and none of it sits behind a form. You can take the templates, use them on Monday and never hear from me again — which is the arrangement I'd want if I were the one downloading.

There's a newsletter at the bottom of this page. It's optional and it unlocks nothing, because a resource you have to trade an email address for isn't free. It's just priced in a currency that's harder to see on the way in.

## The tools

**[Cost of bad data calculator](/en/calculator/)** puts a defensible number on what your current data problems cost per year. It runs in the browser and nothing is sent anywhere. This is usually the fastest route to a governance conversation with a budget attached, because it turns "our data is bad" into a figure a CFO can argue with — and a CFO who is arguing with your number is a CFO who is engaged with your problem.

**[Maturity assessment](/en/maturity-assessment/)** is a practical DAMA-based scorecard. About fifteen minutes, and it comes back with your maturity level, a five-axis radar chart and three quick wins aimed at your weakest pillar. Start here if you know something is wrong but can't yet name it in a sentence.

**[Template library](/en/templates/)** holds the Excel and Word files I actually open on client work: pain-point identification, a 5W2H governance framing, governance evolution stages, catalog tool use cases, principles and policies, and a governance scorecard. They have blank columns on purpose.

**[Playbooks](/en/playbooks/)** are two PDFs — one on standing up data governance, one on AI governance — plus the maturity assessment framework as a document you can rewrite in your own words.

**Scenario simulators** are three branching scenarios that put you inside the trade-offs instead of describing them: [Data Governance Day-to-Day](/simulators/en/data-governance-day-to-day/), [Who Owns This?](/simulators/en/data-ownership-conflict/) and [Data Literacy](/simulators/en/data-literacy/). They take about ten minutes each and they are more fun than this sentence suggests. If you want to run them with your own team, on a private leaderboard and rewritten around your systems, that's the [workshop format](/en/workshops/) — and what the public runs have revealed so far is on [the simulator results page](/en/simulator-results/).

## Where to start

If you don't know what's wrong, start with the [maturity assessment](/en/maturity-assessment/). If you know what's wrong and need budget, start with the [calculator](/en/calculator/). If you have budget and need to put something in front of people next week, start with the [templates](/en/templates/).

And if you'd rather read than download, the [glossary](/en/glossary/) defines the vocabulary and the [articles](/en/blog/) work through the arguments properly. Nothing here expires, so there's no rush.
