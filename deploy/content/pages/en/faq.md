Most data governance explanations are written for people who have already decided to buy something. These are written for the conversation before that: the one where somebody senior asks what this actually is, why it is not just IT tidying up, and what happens if we do nothing.

The answers below are the ones I give in first calls, in the same words. They are DAMA-aligned, because that is the body of knowledge I certified against and the one your auditors will recognize, but none of them require you to have read the DMBOK.

## If your question is not here

Two of these come up so often that they have their own tools. If you want to know where your organization actually stands, the [maturity assessment](/en/maturity-assessment/) takes about fifteen minutes and produces a read you can put in front of a committee. If the question is what the current state is costing, the [cost of bad data calculator](/en/calculator/) turns the anecdotes into a number.

For the rest there is the [blog](/en/blog/), which takes on the questions that do not fit in a paragraph, the [glossary](/en/glossary/) for the vocabulary, and the [three simulators](/en/simulator-results/) if you would rather find out by making the decisions yourself.

And if what you need is a specific answer about your organization rather than a general one, [request an advisory session](/en/advisory-sessions/). Thirty minutes, one problem, no proposal attached.
