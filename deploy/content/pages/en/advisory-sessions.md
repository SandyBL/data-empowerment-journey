Not every governance problem needs a consulting engagement. Most of the ones people write to me about need about half an hour with someone who has seen the same problem fail in three other organisations.

That is what this is. You describe the problem, we talk about it once, and you leave with a position you can act on — or with a clear reason why the thing you were about to do would not have worked. It is a paid conversation rather than a sales call, which is precisely what makes it useful: I have no reason to steer you towards a project.

## When a session is the right size

- **The initiative has stalled and you cannot name why.** You have a council that meets, a policy that exists and no observable change in behaviour. Half an hour is usually enough to find the missing accountability.
- **You are about to choose an operating model and want a second opinion.** Federated, centralised, hub-and-spoke — the terms are cheap and the consequences are not. Bring the org chart.
- **A definition dispute has escalated.** Two functions, two numbers, one meeting next week. There is a standard way out of this and it is not a data model.
- **You took the [maturity assessment](/en/maturity-assessment/) and disagree with the result**, or agree with it and do not know which of the gaps to open first.
- **You have been asked to justify governance to a finance function.** Bring the [calculator](/en/calculator/) output and we will turn it into an argument that survives a CFO.
- **You are an internal governance lead with nobody to think out loud with.** This is the most common reason people come back.

## How it works

You send me the problem in writing first — a few sentences is enough, and the form below prefills the framing. I read it before we speak, so the thirty minutes are spent on the answer rather than on context.

I reply with times that work across your time zone and mine, usually within two working days. **You are requesting a session, not picking a slot from a calendar** — I keep a small number of these open each week and I would rather confirm one that suits you than publish a grid that is out of date by Tuesday.

The session runs remotely, in English, Spanish or Portuguese. Afterwards you get a short written follow-up: what we concluded, what I would do first, and any references worth reading. That note is yours to forward, which matters when the person who needs convincing was not in the room.

## What it is not

It is not a discovery call for a larger project. If the answer to your problem is genuinely a multi-week engagement I will say so, and the [consulting page](/en/consulting/) describes those shapes — but the session stands on its own and most of them end there.

It is not a document review service. I can look at one artefact if you send it in advance, but I cannot read an operating model, a policy set and a data quality report and still have time to talk about them.

It is not training. If what you need is to get a group of people to the same baseline, that is a [workshop](/en/workshops/), and it is a different price and a different shape.

## Pricing shape

A single session is a fixed fee, confirmed in the reply before anything is scheduled. If you decide afterwards that you want continuing help, the fee comes off the first invoice of whatever we agree — advisory, diagnostic or workshop.
