Glossary

Data owner

The single accountable person for a data domain — the one who decides, approves and answers for it, and who has the budget to act.

Also called Domain owner, data trustee, accountable executive

A data owner is senior enough that their decisions stick. They approve definitions, authorise access, accept or reject quality tolerances, and carry the consequence when the domain's data fails somebody downstream. Ownership is accountability, not custody: the owner rarely touches the data and almost never fixes it personally. What distinguishes a real owner from a nominal one is that they can say no to a request from another function and be respected.

In practice. Ownership sits with the business leader whose objectives depend most on the domain — commercial for customer, operations for product, HR for employee. It is one named person, never a team and never a committee, and it appears in their objectives rather than in a slide.

Where it goes wrong. Ownership gets assigned to IT, which has custody but no authority over meaning, or spread across a "working group", which means the next disputed definition has nobody to settle it. The clearest symptom is a request to change a definition that circulates for six weeks: that is not a slow process, it is an absent owner.

Related terms

  • Data stewardThe person who does the day-to-day governance work in a domain: definitions, rules, quality issues, and the questions nobody else can answer.
  • Data stewardshipThe practice and the network of people through which governance decisions actually reach the data.
  • Data domainA bounded subject area of data — customer, product, employee, contract — that one accountable owner can reasonably answer for.
  • Decision rightsThe explicit record of which role decides, which is consulted, and which is merely informed — per decision, not in general.
