DAMA-DMBOK

DAMA International’s reference framework for data management: eleven knowledge areas arranged around data governance at the centre.

Also called DMBOK, Data Management Body of Knowledge

The Data Management Body of Knowledge is the closest thing the profession has to a shared textbook. It defines eleven knowledge areas — governance, architecture, modelling, storage and operations, security, integration and interoperability, document and content, reference and master data, warehousing and business intelligence, metadata, and data quality — and draws them as a wheel with governance at the hub. It is a vocabulary and a checklist, not a methodology: it tells you what exists and what each part is for, and leaves the sequencing to you.

In practice. Its most useful function is as a gap map. Run a programme against the eleven areas and the two or three nobody owns become visible immediately, which is usually more informative than any maturity score. It also settles arguments about words, which is why the CDMP certification is built on it.

Where it goes wrong. Teams treat the wheel as a plan and try to stand up eleven capabilities at once. The wheel is a map of the territory, not a route through it. A first year that does governance, metadata and quality properly on one domain beats a first year that opens eleven workstreams and finishes none.

Related terms

  • Data governanceThe system of decision rights and accountabilities for data — who may decide what about which data, and on what basis.
  • Data managementThe work of building and running the systems, processes, and controls that hold data through its whole life.
  • Data maturity modelA structured scale for rating data capabilities, usually from ad hoc to optimised, used to diagnose where to invest.
