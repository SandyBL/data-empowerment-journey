Glossary

Data literacy

The ability to read, question, interpret and argue with data well enough to make a decision with it.

Also called Data fluency, data skills

Data literacy is not statistics. It is the working capability to look at a chart and know what question it answers, to notice that the denominator changed, to ask where a number came from before repeating it, and to say "this does not support that conclusion" in a meeting. Most of it is scepticism and vocabulary rather than technique, which is why it can be taught to a commercial team in weeks and why a tool rollout never produces it.

In practice. Literacy is built on the organisation's own data, in the context of decisions people already make. A one-hour session that walks a sales team through their own pipeline report — what each field means, where it breaks, which comparisons are invalid — outperforms a generic course by a wide margin.

Where it goes wrong. Literacy is bought as a licence for an e-learning platform and reported as completion rates. Completion measures attendance, not capability. The measure that matters is whether the questions asked in review meetings get better, and that is observable within a quarter if anyone is watching for it.

Related terms

  • Data cultureThe set of habits that determine whether evidence changes what an organisation does — including when it contradicts the person in charge.
  • Data-driven decision makingMaking choices where the evidence is examined before the conclusion is formed, and can change it.
  • Business glossaryThe agreed, owned definitions of the terms the organisation reports on — one meaning per term, with a name against it.
  • Data governanceThe system of decision rights and accountabilities for data — who may decide what about which data, and on what basis.
