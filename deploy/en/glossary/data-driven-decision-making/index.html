Data-driven decision making

Making choices where the evidence is examined before the conclusion is formed, and can change it.

Also called Evidence-based decision making, DDDM

The test is not whether data appears in the decision paper. It is whether the data could have produced a different decision. An organisation that assembles evidence after choosing is not data-driven, it is data-decorated, and everybody involved knows the difference. Being genuinely data-driven also requires accepting that some decisions have insufficient evidence and saying so, rather than dressing a judgement call as an analysis.

In practice. Write down, before the analysis, what result would change your mind. That single habit converts a confirmation exercise into a decision, and it is free.

Where it goes wrong. The phrase is used to mean "we have dashboards". Dashboards are supply. A decision-making culture is demand, and the two are independent — plenty of organisations have excellent reporting that no decision has ever depended on. Governance work that only improves supply will not move this at all.

Related terms

  • Data cultureThe set of habits that determine whether evidence changes what an organisation does — including when it contradicts the person in charge.
  • Data literacyThe ability to read, question, interpret and argue with data well enough to make a decision with it.
  • Single source of truthThe one place designated as authoritative for a given piece of data, which everything else derives from or defers to.
  • Data qualityThe degree to which data is fit for the purpose someone is using it for — which means quality is always relative to a use.
