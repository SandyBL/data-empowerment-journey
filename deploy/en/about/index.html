The person behind the framework

I am Sandy Bradbury, a CDMP-certified data governance consultant. I design governance that survives contact with the organisation it is meant to govern.

Last updated September 5, 2026

Hello. I am Sandy Bradbury, and I specialise in designing and implementing data governance frameworks that are robust, practical and oriented to the business — aligned to the international DAMA standard, but never delivered as a copy of it.

My methodological approach goes beyond technology. I believe the real work is in change management and in structuring the dynamics of the people who touch data every day. A governance framework is only as good as the number of people who behave differently because of it, which is a very different success criterion from the number of policies published.

What I actually do

Most of my work is one of four things: writing the operating model that says who decides what about which data, getting a business glossary to the point where it settles arguments instead of collecting terms, building a data quality practice that measures what somebody will act on, and running the workshops that make the first three stick.

The common thread is that I work on the decisions, not the diagrams. An organisation rarely needs a better target-state architecture. It usually needs one named person who can approve a definition, one forum where cross-domain conflicts get resolved, and one written record of what was decided. That is unglamorous and it is what changes the outcome.

Certifications and DAMA

I hold the CDMP (Certified Data Management Professional) credentials from DAMA International — the Associate level and the Data Management Fundamentals examination. DAMA is the professional body behind the DMBOK, the reference framework most large governance programmes are measured against, and the certification is its practitioner standard.

I mention this for one reason: when a governance recommendation is contested inside an organisation, it helps that the framework behind it is an international standard with a body of practice behind it, rather than one consultant's preference. The DMBOK is a starting point, not a delivery plan — but it is a starting point everyone in the room can check.

Why this site exists

I kept giving the same explanations. What data governance is and is not. Why a data owner has to be a business leader with budget. Why a maturity score is a diagnosis rather than an objective. Why the catalogue that nobody populated is not the tool's fault.

So this site is where I write them down properly: articles, a glossary, free templates and playbooks, a cost-of-bad-data calculator, and three scenario simulators that put a visitor inside the trade-offs instead of describing them. Everything on it is published in English, Spanish and Portuguese, because governance conversations happen in the language of the people having them.

The consulting is downstream of that. If the writing is useful, some readers will want the work done rather than explained — and if it is not useful, the writing was still worth doing.

Working with me

I work remotely, across three languages, usually alongside an internal lead rather than in place of one. Engagements run from a two-week diagnostic to a multi-quarter programme; the shapes are described on the consulting page, and the workshop format — including the private, branded simulator spaces — is on workshops.

If you want to talk, the contact form reaches me directly, and I am on LinkedIn.
