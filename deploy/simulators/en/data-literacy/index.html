Data Literacy & Culture Glossary

Consult key concepts, cognitive biases, governance, and artificial intelligence.

Survivorship Bias

Cognitive Biases

The tendency to focus exclusively on individuals or datasets that "survived" a selection process, ignoring cases that dropped out or failed because they remain hidden or inaccessible.

Confirmation Bias

Cognitive Biases

Selectively cherry-picking metrics and charts that confirm pre-existing hypotheses or political narratives, while dismissing or suppressing contradictory data.

First-Conclusion Bias

Cognitive Biases

Fixating executive decision-making on the first piece of data or explanation offered during a crisis without investigating underlying variables or external factors.

About this simulator

The Data Literacy Simulator is a free, fifteen-scenario exercise about the organisational side of data literacy: not whether an individual can read a chart, but whether a company can act on what its data says. You play the executive making calls on training, tooling, incentives, and escalation, and each decision moves four numbers — data asset value, information ROI, data friction, and a literacy score.

Scenarios are scored across five competencies: governance, cognitive biases, AI and security, root-cause reasoning, and culture. The framing is deliberate. Most data literacy programmes are measured by course completions, which is the one metric that cannot fail. This one measures what happens to the value of the data estate and to the share of the week people spend finding and fixing data instead of using it.

It takes roughly fifteen minutes, keeps an audit trail of every decision you made, and ends with a profile explaining where your choices built capability and where they bought activity. There is no sign-up, and the only thing that can leave your browser is a leaderboard entry you choose to publish.

What you practise

  • Cognitive biases in decision-making — confirmation bias, survivorship, and the dashboard that only ever confirms the strategy.
  • Root-cause reasoning — telling a data problem from a process problem that data is merely reporting.
  • Governance literacy — knowing which questions need an owner before they need an analyst.
  • AI and security judgement — what can responsibly be handed to a model, and what cannot.
  • Data culture — incentives, psychological safety, and why people quietly keep their own spreadsheet.
  • Measuring literacy as a capability — asset value, information ROI, and data friction instead of attendance.

Questions about this simulator

What is data literacy, in one sentence?

Data literacy is the ability to read, interpret, question, and act on data in the context of a real decision — and at organisational level, the ability to do that reliably enough that people trust the answer more than their instinct.

Why does this simulator measure money rather than test scores?

Because a literacy programme that produces certificates but does not reduce the time people spend reconciling numbers has not changed anything. Data asset value, information ROI, and data friction are proxies for whether the capability actually landed.

Is it useful for someone who is not an executive?

Yes. The scenarios are written from a leadership seat because that is where the trade-offs are visible, but analysts and stewards use it to see why the requests they receive look the way they do.

How long does it take and is it free?

About fifteen minutes, free, no account required. Every decision is scored in your browser and the audit trail stays on your device. Publishing to the leaderboard is optional and the only thing sent to us: the display name you type, your final score and how long you took to answer, which then appear on the same global board every other player sees.
